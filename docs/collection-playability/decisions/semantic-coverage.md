# Boundary-level semantic coverage

The capability compiler can return early after recognizing a wheel, blink, trigger, or resource event. Its final unparsed-clause check can suppress unknowns when a mana resource event was found. Resource extraction recognizes individual events and the ramp evaluator may return an empty profile without a completeness signal. The card-advantage compiler supports a deliberately narrow set of fixed-draw shapes. Interaction extraction warns for some unresolved clauses but does not claim to parse every other clause. Oracle paragraph IDs and exact evidence spans do not prove that an ability's costs, targets, and dependencies were fully interpreted.

CPA therefore uses a versioned boundary-level assessment. Only explicit complete shapes with matching native facts qualify as verified. All other structures retain their source and native evidence with partial or unknown coverage. This is a bounded semantic interpretation and does not establish general rules completeness.

The currently verified subset comprises fixed draws, targeted player or opponent mill, their supported combined spell, fixed-symbol mana production, paid self-return from graveyard to battlefield, simple targeted destruction, and two explicit triggered-damage shapes. Activated shapes require fixed mana or tap/untap symbol costs. Additional costs, linked effects, modes, and conditional text outside those exact shapes remain partial or unknown. A general extractor unknown may be resolved by the Interaction engine only for the exact supported targeted-destruction shape.

CPA preserves boundary identity, card and face, exact source span, native evidence references, unknown spans, recognized activation cost, targeting and conditions, state, reason codes, and verifier version. `verified` is an allowlisted interpretation backed by matching native facts. It is neither a character-coverage metric nor a claim that the native engine fully understands the card. Any unverified boundary makes its associated CPA uses unknown and prevents strategy context from that evidence.

Remaining risks include restrictions in separate ability boundaries, rules encoded in reminder text or keyword abilities, and effects not recognized by any engine. The resolver and per-boundary verifier do not establish whether an independent static ability modifies another ability. Consumers must preserve unknown coverage and avoid treating a verified local shape as proof of whole-card completeness.

Each `MechanicalUse` exposes a safety state. Supported requires a resolved execution unit and general applicability. Conditional requires a resolved unit and stated conditions or restrictions. Unsupported includes uses with unknown applicability or no execution identity. Strategy contexts are derived only from uses inside that envelope.

An unverified separate boundary is treated as a possible dependency unless a narrow restriction is explicitly scoped or a standalone supported keyword is isolated. Conditional or cross-face modifiers do not become always-active rules. Their potentially affected uses instead abstain. Boundary resolution and the modifier detector still have finite coverage, and unknown source structure can lead to false abstention. Additional independent review is needed before treating this envelope as proof of general Magic rules completeness.

## Boundary-scoped native evidence

The native resource and capability extractors can associate Oracle evidence with a whole physical line. When that line contains more than one activated ability, the span cannot identify which ability supplied the effect. CPA can re-run native mechanical and ramp extraction on each complete boundary of a single-face paragraph when the resolver finds at least two distinct activations, the partition covers the paragraph apart from whitespace, and native evidence crosses the partition. Quoted, parenthesized, mixed-type, overlapping and ambiguous partitions stay on the existing conservative path. Multi-face cards also stay on that path.

Scoped extraction uses the same catalog and card metadata. It preserves the original parser paragraph ID and translates local offsets back to the original Oracle snapshot. Evidence identifies the scope by boundary ID and records the parser version. The scoped extraction bypasses parser and evaluation caches. Existing unscoped evidence IDs remain stable.

Verification and dependency checks run after re-extraction. Original whole-line warnings remain visible. An exact allowlisted shape with a matching scoped native fact can supersede an overlapping whole-line warning for that boundary, while a warning produced inside the ability continues through the existing verification rules. Verified tap and untap costs flow into the mechanical use and its strategy prerequisites. Compound modifiers can receive a narrow dependency scope only when their entire source matches a supported narrow restriction. Other modifier shapes conservatively affect every plausible use. The Interaction and card-advantage engines continue to supply their existing evidence, with CPA abstaining when execution identity or prerequisites remain uncertain.
