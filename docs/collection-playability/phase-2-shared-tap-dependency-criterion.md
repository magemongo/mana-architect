# CPA Phase 2 — Shared tap dependency acceptance criterion

**Full Phase 2 release status — BLOCKED.** Sections 5.3 and 11 of the implementation guide require peer-relative effect costs, contextual valuation of combined effects, timing, reliability, repeatability, resource constraints and transparent explanations. The gate requires detecting Thassa's Bounty-like inefficiency while preserving the value of expensive, high-impact effects. A criterion for one activation prerequisite covers only part of that work.

## Proposed criterion

**Record shared tap competition only when two distinct, resolved activated boundaries on the same card and face have an explicit `{T}` as a required payment in their respective, exact pre-colon cost spans.** Confirm both symbols refer to the same source permanent and that neither is quoted, optional, an alternative payment, part of an effect, or attached to another object. Use the pinned card ID, face, boundary IDs and offsets to establish the association. The draw boundary must independently satisfy the [mana and tap contract](mana-tap-activation-contract.md), including its matching native draw fact and execution unit. The other ability's complete effect need not be verified to report the shared printed prerequisite. Uncertainty about which cost or source a symbol belongs to prevents a positive shared-tap finding.

Both payments consume the same untapped state. After one tap payment, a later tap payment requires the source to become able to tap again. Mana, control, creature tap restrictions, priority and permission apply separately to each activation. A shared tap finding gives no activation count or order, opportunity-cost estimate, efficiency rating or strategy context. Effects that can restore tap availability require separate evidence even when the card also has shared tap payments.

**Treat a direct modifier as a separate finding.** Require source evidence of an effect or instruction that can change the draw quantity or resolution, or change this ability's activation cost, timing or permission. Bind the modified event, action or object to the draw activation and preserve any conditions on when the modifier applies. For example, a printed prohibition on drawing applies to the draw event even though the activation itself is locally verified. A cost to tap the source changes its state rather than changing the printed draw instruction or printed payment. A context-dependent effect with an unknown target or payload leaves its possible influence unresolved. Failure to match a known modifier pattern supplies no evidence of independence.

For a future policy to treat a pair as **shared tap only within the printed card**, require positive coverage of both complete effects. Start with two bound, fixed, unconditional, untargeted draw effects and fully verified costs. Each effect must match its complete supported shape and native evidence. The remaining card-level check must still reject any unresolved relevant activation instruction, external-effect delegation, ambiguous association, relevant other boundary or modifier. Model shared competition without a downgrade only when this limited proof succeeds and the other applicability gates also pass. The claim is confined to the supplied printed snapshot. Effects elsewhere in a game can change draw or activation, so independence in an actual game remains outside the claim.

## League Guildmage and current behavior

The pinned record has two resolved activations. Draw is `[0:25]` with `{3}{U}, {T}`. Copy is `[26:147]` with `{X}{R}, {T}`. Both costs provide positive evidence of shared tap competition. The copy clause targets an instant or sorcery spell you control with mana value X and permits new targets. The selected spell and its effects depend on the game state. Its printed clause provides no direct modification of the printed draw, and does not constrain the copy's eventual effects enough to establish the closed pair described above. **Retain card-level abstention on the intact card.** A future shared tap annotation could coexist with the unresolved spell-copy dependency. It would leave the draw's local verification intact while retaining `card_dependency_unresolved` on its use. [League Guildmage diagnostic](phase-2-league-guildmage-dependency.md)

`assess_dependencies` skips verified boundaries and lets a nonverified boundary with no recognized narrower rule fall back to all other capability groups. It records an `unknown` dependency with `card_dependency_unresolved`. `evaluate_mechanics` uses the resulting affected set to retain draw evidence while setting applicability to `unknown`. Removing just the copy clause in the synthetic control leaves the draw conditional and isolates the fallback as the cause of the downgrade. [Dependency policy](decisions/card-dependencies.md)

## Evidence and representation for review

- `BoundaryCoverage` already records card and face, exact Oracle bounds and text, boundary ID, activation cost and recognized evidence IDs. Cite both boundaries for a shared tap finding. Re-derive each cost's original subspan from its boundary and colon and validate the source slice. A native draw reference must remain tied to the draw boundary.
- `ExecutionUnit` carries activation cost, identity and evidence. `MechanicalUse` carries the unit ID, `pay_ability_cost`, `tap_source`, applicability and safety. Retain those requirements for the identified draw unit. The copy boundary needs an unambiguous source cost for the printed shared-tap finding even if it lacks a verified execution use.
- `CardDependency` can point from a source boundary and exact span to an affected boundary. A future additive `shared_tap_prerequisite` category could use `potentially_affected`, `conditional` and `same_face`, with a reason identifying the shared source payment. Its meaning would be a resource interaction and it would not add an affected ID to the mechanics blocking set on its own. The directed record can be read with both cost spans as evidence of a symmetric constraint. Keep the existing unknown dependency category, reason and blocking entry when the other effect is unresolved. A true modifier cites the modifier's full scope-bearing clause and the affected draw boundary. A later-availability finding cites the untap clause and its object binding to the source. Neither annotation should overwrite a direct restriction. No current field records tap history, a copied spell's resolved payload, or actual game-state activation opportunities. Withhold conclusions that need those facts.
